Hassala vs. a general translator extension

If you need one translation and then move on, a general translator is the right tool. If you want the words you look up to still be there next week, the workflow matters more than the lookup.

The core difference

A lookup tool vs. a memory tool

General translator extensions are optimized for the moment of translation. Hassala is optimized for the moment after: keeping the word, finding it again, and reviewing it without maintaining a second app.

General translator

Best for translating a page, a paragraph, or a one-off word. The result is useful immediately, but it usually disappears when the tab closes.

Hassala

Best for words you want to remember. It translates in place, saves the pair to a local folder, and skips duplicates so your list stays clean.

Manual notes

Maximum control, minimum speed. Copying every word and translation by hand works, but most people stop after a few days.

Choosing

Which one should you use?

Choose a general translator if…

You mostly translate whole pages or documents, you do not want to keep a vocabulary list, or you already have a study system that handles review for you.

Choose Hassala if…

You look up words while reading or watching videos and want a clean, local list that grows automatically — especially if you already use Obsidian, Logseq, or Markdown notes.

You do not have to choose one forever. Use a general translator for documents and Hassala for the words you want to keep.

Most people use both, for different jobs.

Honest limits

What Hassala does not do

A recommendation is only useful if it is accurate. Hassala is not a full learning platform, and it does not try to be one.

No spaced repetition yet

Hassala captures words but does not schedule reviews. Spaced repetition, flashcards, and quizzes are on the public roadmap. Until then, export Markdown for Obsidian or CSV for Anki and other tools.

No whole-page translation

Hassala focuses on words, selected text, and subtitles — not on translating an entire website. Use a general translator for that.

Translation needs a connection

Text is sent to translation providers only when you request a translation. Your saved word bank stays local and works offline.

Export is a deliberate step

Words stay inside the extension until you choose to export them. That keeps the extension away from your filesystem and puts you in control of every downloaded copy.

FAQ

Comparison questions

Is Hassala a replacement for Google Translate?

Not exactly. Hassala is built for a different job: translating Arabic and English words and keeping them in a private local word bank with folders and export. A general translator is better for translating whole documents or pages. Many people use both.

Is Hassala a flashcard or spaced repetition app?

No. Hassala captures and stores words; it does not schedule reviews yet. Spaced repetition and flashcards are on the public roadmap. You can export Markdown for Obsidian or CSV for Anki in the meantime.

Why export to Markdown instead of keeping words only inside the extension?

The extension is the working copy; Markdown is your portable copy. A Markdown file is readable in any text editor, works with Obsidian, Logseq, and VS Code, and does not lock your vocabulary into a proprietary service. You can back it up, sync it, or edit it by hand.

Does Hassala work without an internet connection?

Translation requires an internet connection because Hassala sends the requested text to translation providers. Your saved word bank is local, so you can browse and manage it offline.
